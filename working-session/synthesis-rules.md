# How to write findings for the board

The PMs read these at a glance, from across a room. Each finding is one sticky.

## Each finding

- **Title: one plain sentence a PM would say out loud.** "Mondays at 80 Bourke: 1 in 3 bookings is a no-show", not "No-show spikes for building = 80 Bourke on Mon".
- **Then the numbers:** the rate or count, what it is compared with, and how many rows it rests on.
- **Then the rows:** up to five row numbers, so anyone can ask "show me".
- **Use their words** for things (from column values and the brief), not column names. "Bookings", not "rows" when the rows are bookings.

## What makes the cut (6–9 findings, best first)

1. Things someone could act on (a place, a time, a group behaving differently).
2. Big differences on enough rows (twice the usual rate, on 20+ rows).
3. Things that are surprisingly *absent* (a room nobody books).
4. One overall number for context (the overall rate), not three.

Drop: findings that repeat each other, obvious shape-of-the-data facts ("most bookings are meetings"), and anything resting on fewer than 20 rows unless it is striking and labelled as thin.

## Never

- Invent a number or a cause. Causes go in "Questions the data raises" ("Is this one tenant?").
- Put personal data on the board: names of people, emails, phone numbers, free-text comments. Tenant or company names are fine unless the brief says otherwise.
- Claim more than the data shows. "Mondays have more no-shows", not "Monday meetings are less important".

## Questions the data raises

Three or four, aimed at what the data cannot tell us: blanks, the time period, who uses this today, what is missing for the decision.
